The file here is the pack's FBX/Trex.fbx turned into glTF binary with three.js
itself (0.186): read with three/addons/loaders/FBXLoader.js and written with
three/addons/exporters/GLTFExporter.js. Its mesh, skeleton and all six of its
clips (TRex_Walk, TRex_Run, TRex_Idle, TRex_Attack, TRex_Jump, TRex_Death,
each named "Armature|…") are as the FBX has them, with three changes:

- its faces are put in order of material, so the mesh is drawn in five parts
  rather than 166;
- each of its five materials (Green, Black, LightGreen, Red, LightYellow) is a
  plain glTF material of the FBX's diffuse color, fully rough and not metal.
  The FBX gives each an emissive color equal to its diffuse one at nought
  strength, which glTF cannot say, so that is left out rather than made to
  glow;
- the few vertices the FBX weights to more than four bones keep their four
  strongest, as glTF holds four.

The walk measured on the converted file — how far a stride carries the
creature, and how wide and how high its body is along its length — comes
within 0.3 % of the same measured on the FBX itself.

No credit is asked for. The example credits the model in the viewer's
credits line anyway, while it is shown.
